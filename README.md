# Bangladesh-Students-Performance
This project analyzes student performance data from Bangladesh to identify the factors that influence academic achievement. The analysis explores relationships between socioeconomic background, study habits, school characteristics, and previous academic performance to uncover patterns that contribute to student success.

## Objectives

- Analyze the factors influencing student academic performance.
- Examine the relationship between family income and HSC results.
- Evaluate the impact of study hours on academic achievement.
- Compare student performance across different school types.
- Identify top-performing students based on SSC and GPA records.
- Explore the relationship between previous academic performance and HSC outcomes.
- Generate actionable insights to support educational decision-making.

## Key Insights

- Students from higher-income families generally achieved better HSC results, indicating a positive relationship between socioeconomic status and academic performance.

- Increased study hours per week were associated with improved HSC outcomes, highlighting the importance of consistent study habits.

- Significant differences in average HSC results were observed across various school types, suggesting that educational environments influence student achievement.

- Previous GPA demonstrated a strong positive correlation with HSC results, making it a reliable indicator of future academic performance.

- Students with strong SSC results consistently performed well in HSC examinations, emphasizing the importance of a solid academic foundation.

- The overall distribution of HSC results revealed noticeable performance variations among students, indicating the need for targeted academic support.

- District-level analysis identified disparities in academic achievement, suggesting regional differences in educational resources and opportunities.

- Top-performing students maintained consistently high academic records across multiple educational stages.

- Students with higher previous GPAs were more likely to achieve outstanding HSC results compared to those with lower GPAs.
